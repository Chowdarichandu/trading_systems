# STAGGERED-ENTRY TEST — EKAM INTRADAY CANDIDATES · 2026-11-17

**Owner's question (2026-11-17 10:12 IST):** this morning all 8 live EKAM entries
fired at 09:20 off the same 09:15 bar and three stopped/decayed together within
the hour — *what if we take positions in different time intervals? How would they
impact our loss proportion? Can you do that?*

**Answer in one line: staggering spreads the losses out in time, but it does not
reduce them — the losing share of trades goes UP in 10 of 12 book-years, the
profit falls to 13–97% of the control, and NO variant passes the frozen bar for
either book.** The pile-up days disappear; the money does not come back.

## MECHANISM — does staggering decorrelate the losses, or just trade less?

**It does not even trade less.** The daily caps bind on nearly every day under
every variant (Cand-1: 1,025–1,035 trades vs control 1,025–1,035; Cand-2:
615–621 vs 615–621) — there are almost always enough qualifying names later in
the day to refill the cap. Staggering swaps *which* trades are taken, not how
many. What it changes:

1. **The pile-up really does disappear.** Cluster days (≥3 stops in a day) fall
   from 10/9 (Cand-1 control, 2025/2026) to 0–3 under every variant, and the
   money booked on those days shrinks from −₹21,803 / −₹37,284 to near zero.
   Entries at different times stop and decay at different times — the
   "everything died together by 10:30" shape is gone.
2. **But the losses themselves are not decorrelated — they are slightly worse.**
   Losing share rises in 10 of 12 book-years (Cand-1: 64.4% → 66–70%). Each
   individual loss gets a little smaller (average loss −₹596 → −₹455…−₹496 on
   Cand-1), yet there are more losers and the winners shrink faster than the
   losers do, so the net falls everywhere and ex-top-3 goes negative in 9 of 12
   cells.
3. **Why:** the books' edge is concentrated in the *earliest* qualifying bars —
   the first ≥5-vote reading catches the move near its start, leaving room
   before the votes decay. A name still showing ≥5 votes at 10:20 or 11:20 has
   usually already travelled; entering there buys the same move with less room
   left, so the decay exit and the −1% stop arrive sooner relative to the
   remaining move. Delay is a cost, not a hedge. LATE is the extreme proof:
   skipping the morning entirely turns Cand-1's 2025 negative (−₹32,599.80).
4. **Drawdown does not reliably improve either** — it improves in some cells
   (Cand-2 SLOT60 2025: 64% of control) and worsens in others (Cand-1 SLOT30
   2026: 123% of control). Spreading entries reshuffles *when* the pain lands;
   it does not cap it.

**Bottom line for the owner's question:** the loss *proportion* — the share of
trades that lose — is not improved by taking positions at different time
intervals; it gets marginally worse, because the early entries are the good
ones. What staggering genuinely fixes is only the *simultaneity* — the bad
feeling of three stops in one hour — and it charges most of the book's profit
for that comfort. The frozen live candidates stay as they are; nothing in this
test is armed.

control has zero cluster days — today's (Nov 17) live pile-up is a live-layer
day outside that window, which is exactly the pattern this test was built to
price.
