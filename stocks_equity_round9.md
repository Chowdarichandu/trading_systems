## Verdict first — NO survivor. The trend gates point the WRONG WAY.

- **No gate survives.** Under the frozen rule (kept set net-positive
  in BOTH halves at ₹40 at the primary exit AND ≥40% of P2 trades
  kept in each half), all five gates and the stack fail.
- **The striking result is an inversion.** The trend-alignment
  gates (G1 Nifty>50DMA, G2 Nifty>20DMA, G3 stock-trend alignment)
  keep the LOSING subset and drop the winning one: their **dropped**
  sets are net-positive in both halves with gross expectancies of
  +₹73 to +₹126/trade, while their kept sets are deeply negative
  in Jan–May. The P2 edge — a SHORT-heavy morning re-entry pool —
  lives on days when Nifty sits BELOW its trend averages and on
  counter-trend stock setups, not in aligned uptrends. This is
  reported as a finding, **not** flipped into a rule: an inverse
  gate ("trade only below the 50DMA") would be a hypothesis
  generated by this same data, and claiming it from the data that
  suggested it would be fitting. It can only be tested on a fresh
  frozen window.
- **G4 (volatility mid-band) is the only gate whose kept set is
  net-positive in both halves at the primary exit** (+₹419.47 /
  +₹4,630.51) — but it keeps only **20.8%** of Jan–May trades,
  failing the frozen ≥40% sample rule. A near-flat first half on
  141 trades is not a survivor.
- **The Jan–May hole is not a regime the gates can see.** It is
  one catastrophic month (Feb, −₹29,912.27, gross expectancy
  −₹125.26) plus April (−₹9,593.21), largely offset inside the
  same half by March (+₹30,824.62) — and within Jan–May it is the
  **SHORT side** that loses (−₹20,220.44) while LONGs pay
  (+₹15,184.94). No ex-ante daily-bar state tested here separates
  February from March.

## Verdict

1. **No survivor, no paper spec earned.** All five gates and the
   stack fail the frozen rule at the primary exit. Nothing is
   adopted; nothing from this round is flagged for a frozen paper
   spec. N4-V3's November paper is unaffected.
2. **The regime hypothesis, as posed, is answered — negatively
   for alignment gates.** Jan–May's failure is not concentrated
   in an above/below-trend state that a pre-day indicator can
   switch off: alignment gates remove the pool's best subset.
   The pool's gross edge concentrates on below-trend days in
   BOTH halves (G1/G2/G3 dropped sets), which inverts the
   question — but the inverse gate is a hypothesis this data
   generated, and under the standing discipline it earns at most
   a pre-declared test on fresh data, never a claim from
   Jan–Oct itself.
3. **G4 remains a weak observation:** its T125 kept set is the
   only both-halves-net-positive kept set in nine rounds of gate
   and filter testing, but on 20.8% of the first-half sample and
   it flips negative at T10. Recorded, not carried.
4. **What is now established about P2:** the binding facts are
   (a) February-type entry failure (gross-negative SHORTs) that
   no daily-bar regime state isolates, and (b) a gross edge that
   is real but regime-timed in a way previous-day trend/volatility
   states do not capture. The exit dimension (Round 8) and the
   daily-regime dimension (this round) are both exhausted for
   this pool.
